How it is worked out

With gross margin m, a discount d needs m ÷ (m − d) − 1 extra volume, and a price rise p lets you lose p ÷ (m + p) of volume. Both assume your cost per sale stays the same and nothing else changes. Figures are rounded to the nearest percent.

This compares gross profit before advertising spend. If you run paid media, a discount also moves the return on ad spend you need to break even. That is a separate calculation, and not one this page makes.

Questions

How much extra volume does a 10% discount need?

It depends entirely on your gross margin. At a 50% gross margin, a 10% discount needs 25% more sales volume to make the same gross profit. At a 30% margin, the same 10% discount needs 50% more. The lower the margin, the more punishing the discount.

How much volume can I afford to lose after a price rise?

At a 50% gross margin, a 10% price rise means you could lose 17% of your sales volume and still make the same gross profit. Lose less than that and you are better off, with less work for the same money.

What counts as gross margin?

Sales minus the cost of those sales, expressed as a percentage of sales. For an e-commerce brand that usually means revenue minus product cost, and often minus shipping and payment fees. It is on your profit and loss statement.

Does this include advertising cost?

No. It compares gross profit before advertising spend, and assumes your cost per sale stays the same. If you run paid media, a discount also raises the return on ad spend you need to break even, which this calculator does not show.
