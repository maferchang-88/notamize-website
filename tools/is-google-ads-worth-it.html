Before you spend anything

You have been told Google Ads works. Maybe it does. Nobody has told you whether it works for your business, and that is the only version of the question worth answering.

Most accounts that quietly fail were doomed before a single ad ran: the diary was already full, nobody knew which enquiries turned into customers, or the budget was never going to be enough to learn anything. None of that is a bidding problem, and none of it gets fixed by a better agency.

These are the eight questions I ask on a first call, in the order I ask them. Answer honestly and you will get the same answer I would give you, including the one where I tell you not to spend the money.

Why these eight, and not a cost calculator

A calculator tells you what a sale is allowed to cost. Useful, but it answers a question most businesses are not actually stuck on. The things that decide whether advertising works are duller than arithmetic: whether you have room to take the work, whether you can tell which enquiries became customers, and whether you can change your own website.

Three answers end it on their own, whatever the rest say, and they are the three I have given in real calls:

  • Full all year. Buying enquiries you will turn away is the most expensive mistake on this list. Raise prices or sell more to each customer instead. That is free.
  • Results expected within two weeks. Bidding needs weeks of data before it is doing anything but guessing. Starting with that expectation means stopping before it works and concluding it does not.
  • No tracking and nobody who can edit the site. Without measurement you are not advertising, you are donating. The first job is tracking, and it has to be possible before anything else is worth paying for.

Everything else is scored rather than fatal. "I do not know" is a legitimate answer and is counted honestly: not knowing your close rate is not a disqualification, it is the first thing to fix.
